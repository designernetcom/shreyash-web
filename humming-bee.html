HummingBEE

Adjustable-amplitude bubble CPAP

An additional oscillatory control variable for neonatal bubble CPAP.

HummingBEE is a neonatal bubble CPAP system engineered with an independently controlled secondary extra-flow channel in its bubble-generating chamber. This configuration lets the pressure oscillation excursion (ΔP) be adjusted separately from the primary bias-flow setting across much of the tested operating range.

Characterised in a 2026 peer-reviewed in-vitro study across 32 combinations of primary bias flow and secondary extra flow.

How it works

The engineering behind adjustable-amplitude bubble CPAP

In conventional bubble CPAP, oscillations are generated as gas leaves a submerged expiratory limb. HummingBEE adds a separately controlled gas stream into an enlarged bubble-generating chamber. That additional stream changes bubble interactions and the resulting oscillatory pressure waveform.

Bench testing showed peak-to-peak oscillatory pressure excursion increasing as extra flow increased, while prescribed CPAP was maintained across most measured conditions. Some high-flow combinations produced upward pressure drift.

  • Nominal CPAP set by water-column depth
  • Primary blended-gas / bias-flow path
  • Independent secondary extra-flow channel
  • Bubble-generating chamber
  • Pressure-transducer measurement point
  1. Stage 1

    Set CPAP

    Water-column depth in the generating chamber sets the prescribed pressure.

  2. Stage 2

    Add secondary flow

    A separately controlled stream enters the same chamber and alters bubble behaviour.

  3. Stage 3

    Adjust amplitude

    Measured ΔP rises with extra flow, independently of the primary bias-flow setting.

Published experimental performance

Measured amplitude response across 32 bench-test conditions

Using a fixed-compliance test-lung model, a calibrated airway-pressure transducer and an oscilloscope cross-check, the researchers examined four primary bias-flow levels (5, 6, 7 and 8 L/min) across eight secondary extra-flow settings (0–14 L/min). At 7 L/min primary bias flow, measured ΔP rose from 9.89 to 17.8 cmH₂O as extra flow increased from 0 to 14 L/min, an increase of about 80%.

Modes, controls and monitoring

Three therapy selections, one engineering platform

Product literature describes three therapy selections: conventional bubble CPAP, bubble CPAP with adjustable amplitude, and high-flow nasal therapy.

The front panel indicates delivered CPAP, the measured dominant oscillation frequency in Hz, and the pressure excursion ΔP. The amplitude control is separate from the FiO2 blender and from the primary flow setting.

Scientific research

Published research on the HummingBEE mechanism

A 2026 research article in the International Journal of Health Technology and Innovation reports in-vitro characterisation of HummingBEE's pressure oscillations and describes preliminary observational use in approximately 50 neonates.

The bench study used a simplified fixed-compliance lung model. It did not directly measure gas exchange, tidal volume, carbon-dioxide elimination or patient work of breathing. The authors identify prospective controlled clinical trials as necessary to establish patient outcome benefits.

Full citation

Waghmare S, Waghmare S, Raj AY, Tagare A, Ghirwalker A, Kulkarni M, Parikh T. In-vitro Bench Evaluation of a Novel Adjustable-Amplitude Bubble CPAP System for Neonatal Respiratory Support. IJHTI. 2026;5(2):7–15.

doi.org/10.60142/ijhti.v5i02.03

Disclosure. Company personnel are among the authors. Do not describe this work as independent multicentre validation.

Clinical experience

Early clinical experience and research collaborations

HummingBEE has been evaluated in preliminary neonatal clinical settings. Research and conference presentations include observational experience, an eight-neonate pilot and a non-randomised comparison with conventional bubble CPAP. These early findings inform further clinical research. Definitive comparative clinical benefits have not yet been established.
